Phase 2 — Maturity Assessment

The output is a spider diagram, a scored rollup and a chain (root-cause) analysis — all governed by worst-dimension-wins.

Outputs of this phase — a maturity spider diagram, a scored rollup, and a root-cause (chain) analysis. Every catalogue item is rated 0–5 against a ladder and a set of gates. The item's score is the lowest of them — worst-dimension-wins — and the weakest gate is recorded as the binding dimension, which is where remediation must start.

Where each rule applies — read this before the tables. Worst-dimension-wins operates within a single catalogue item: that item scores the lowest of its ladder rating, its gates and its entity cap. Rolling items up to their element uses the arithmetic mean of the item scores, and the fifteen element scores are then combined by weighted mean into the index. So an item's score reflects its single weakest gate, while an element's score reflects the average condition of everything inside it.

Reading the diagram

The red shape is where the department is; the dashed blue ring is the near-term target of 3.0 ("documented, measured and managed"). The dents tell the story: the Technology and IS-Solution layers collapse inward — Data Access (0.5), Technology Integration (0.75), Technology External (0.75) and Technology Internal (0.78) are the department's structural weak points. The Motivation layer (Outcome, Client, Value Proposition) holds up best at ~2.0.

The pattern is typical: the department knows what it is trying to achieve, but the plumbing that would let it deliver digitally — integration, data exchange, master data — barely exists.

How an item is scored

Item score = MIN( ladder , every gate , entity cap )

A high ladder rating cannot rescue an item with a failing gate. If a service runs on a modern application (ladder 4) but has no owner and no audit trail (gate 1), the item scores 1. That single weakest dimension is the binding dimension — the thing to fix first.

Element rollup & maturity zones

Rolling item scores up to the element gives the departmental scorecard — each element score is the arithmetic mean of its items. All 15 elements land in Intervene in this baseline even though a handful of individual items score "Improve", because too few items clear 2.0 for any element average to lift out of the zone. The item-level rule (worst-dimension-wins) is what pushed most of those items down in the first place.

Standards behind the scoring. Each gate is anchored to an authority — Batho Pele and the Operations Management Framework (service & process), POPIA and MIOS (data exchange & interoperability), ISO/IEC 38500 and COBIT (ICT governance), and the PSR security regulations (cyber maturity). The score is defensible because it is evidence-based, not opinion.
