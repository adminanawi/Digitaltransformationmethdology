Key findings from the baseline

!
No EA function and no in-house skills (D1 — 0.80). Questions 1.1 and 1.5 both scored 0: there is no designated enterprise architect and no TOGAF/ArchiMate competency on staff. Architecture work is occasional and ad-hoc. This is the single biggest blocker — without a capability, GWEA cannot be sustained.
!
An EA exercise has been done but produced no roadmap (D2 — 1.50). The department has conducted an exercise and it is reasonably current (2.2=3), but no implementation roadmap was produced (2.3=0) and no tracked delivery followed (2.4=1). The assessment work has not converted into action.
!
Repository practically empty (D3 — 0.67). A repository location exists and is referenced occasionally (3.1=1, 3.2=1), but it holds no models (3.3=0), no structured data (3.4=0), and no functional descriptions (3.6=0). Technical diagrams exist for some systems (3.5=2) but are unconnected to any governed store.
~
Data exchange still largely manual (D4 — 1.00). A data-management function is defined (4.1=3), a positive foundation. But external exchange is still email/SFTP-heavy (4.3=0), internal systems are not integrated (4.4=1), and internal manual exchange persists (4.5=0). Interoperability via secured APIs is minimal (4.2=1).
~
Governance structure exists but is not enforced (D5 — 1.60). The architecture board scores 4 — it exists and meets regularly (5.1=4). However, GWEA compliance checks are weak (5.2=2), the board is not linked to motivation models (5.3=1), MIOS/OMF standards are not applied (5.4=0), and investment decisions bypass architecture review in practice (5.5=1).
+
Citizen-facing digitisation is the strongest signal (D6 — 2.20). Priority external services are available digitally (6.2=3) and around half are automated (6.3=3). This provides a platform to build on. The gap is a lack of service strategy (6.5=1) and weak internal service coverage (6.1=2).

What this means for the GWEA cycle

This baseline establishes the starting point for the full GWEA 2.0 improvement programme. The department's 1.29 overall score must reach 3.0 — a gap of 1.71 points — to be classified as Defined / FAIR. Phases 1–5 are sequenced to close that gap systematically: Phase 1 (Cataloguing) addresses D3 by populating the repository; Phase 2 (Assessment) deepens D1 and D2 by applying the method in-house; Phase 3 (Roadmap) converts findings into a tracked execution plan (closing D2's 2.3 gap); Phase 4 (Remedial projects) targets D4 integration and D5 alignment; Phase 5 (Governance) institutionalises the board's authority to enforce the scores that currently sit at zero.

Maturity scoring scale. 0 = Non-existent · 1 = Initial (ad-hoc) · 2 = Repeatable (inconsistent) · 3 = Defined — FAIR (target) · 4 = Managed (good) · 5 = Optimised (excellent). These are the questionnaire's own level names, and they sit on the same 0–5 scale used everywhere else on this site. They map to the site-wide zones as follows: 0–2 (Non-existent, Initial, Repeatable) = Intervene · >2–3 (Defined / FAIR) = Improve · >3–4 (Managed) = Good · >4–5 (Optimised) = Excellent. Each dimension score is the arithmetic mean of its questions. The overall maturity score is the equally-weighted average of the six dimension scores. Scores are illustrative training data from the GWEA 2.0 Departmental Maturity Questionnaire applied to a worked example for the Department of Human Settlements.
