Before any roadmap is committed, its ambition is tested against the enabling-environment readiness profile. It asks the blunt question the architecture cannot: even if the future state were designed perfectly, could this department actually reach it? Eleven dimensions, scored 0–4 across five clusters — and one of them is a hard gate.

Current (C2) Target (3)

One spoke stands tall — executive sponsorship (3). Two collapse: procurement agility (1) and, critically, change-and-delivery capability (1). Leadership wants the change; the machinery to buy and build it does not exist.

Hard gate — Dimension 11: NOT CLEARED. There is no standing change-and-delivery capability. Under the practice, where the hard gate is not cleared no ambitious architecture change may be scheduled until it is built. This is why Wave 0 exists — it spends the first six months building delivery capability, clearing the gate before the integration programme it enables is allowed to start. A perfect future state handed to an entity that has never finished anything will not be realised.
