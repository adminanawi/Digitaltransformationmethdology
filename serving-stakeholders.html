A government department does not answer to one master. It sits inside a chain of accountability that runs from the Constitution down to the individual citizen, and every link holds a concern it must satisfy. GWEA 2.0 is the only function that can trace that full line — from a missed outcome down to the architectural defect that caused it — and answer each stakeholder in their own language, from evidence the practice already holds.

One diagnostic, not three reports. When a grant payment fails, the service unit blames the system, IT blames the data, operations blames the process — and the DG receives three reports with no answer. The practice traces the single chain: citizens are not paid because the payment application cannot match identity to grant type, because the master data linking them was never integrated. One finding, one accountable fix, one measurable target — and it answers the citizen, the Minister, the DG, the Auditor-General and Treasury at once.

The one-view answer. Every stakeholder is asking a version of the same question: is this department configured to deliver what it must, and if not, why, and what will it cost to fix? The artefacts linked here are that single answer, published to each audience in the language of the concern they hold. The three views the document also names but that need data we do not hold — the cross-department PEAR Leaderboard and the Auditor-General Pre-Audit Assurance rollup — are deliberately omitted here.
