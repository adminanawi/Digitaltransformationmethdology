Enabling Environment

Enabling Environment — Cataloguing & Assessment

The enabling environment is catalogued and then assessed — a register of what exists per dimension, followed by a readiness spider, a five-cluster rollup and the hard-gate verdict that governs the roadmap.

Two outputs, mirroring the architecture. The Enabling Environment is not one of the five phases; it runs underneath all of them. But it is worked exactly like the architecture: first catalogued (a Phase-1-style register of what exists per readiness dimension), then assessed (a Phase-2-style readiness rating). It answers a blunt question: even if the architecture were perfect, could this department actually deliver the change?

Output 1 — Enabling Environment catalogue

Before any rating, the readiness dimensions are inventoried the same way the 15 architecture elements are. Eleven dimensions sit in five clusters; each is populated with the concrete artefacts, mandates and mechanisms the department actually has (or is found to be missing).

Mandate & Money

  • 1 · Policy & regulatory reform — Housing Act 107 of 1997 · PIE Act · PSR delegations & CGICTPF adoption · MIOS mandate (PSR Reg 97) · provincial housing legislation
  • 5 · Funding & financial sustainability — HSDG grant line · USDG grant line · departmental ICT budget line · MTEF three-year allocation · APP budget programme structure
  • 6 · Procurement & supply-chain agility — SITA mandated procurement channel · departmental SCM policy · transversal term contracts · tender / bid register · innovation-procurement route (not yet in place)

Leadership

  • 4 · Executive sponsorship & political will — Director-General digital mandate · ICT governance charter · GITO / DG steering forum · standing transformation steering committee

People & Culture

  • 2 · Workforce & skills — IT / EA organisational structure · competency framework & job profiles · vacancy & skills-gap register · NSG / CSIR training plan · contractor / SLA panel
  • 10 · Change appetite & culture — organisational change-management approach · Batho Pele culture programme · adoption / uptake metrics
  • 11 · Change & delivery capability HARD GATE — programme / project office (PMO) charter (absent) · delivery method / SOPs · project register · key-person dependency log

Data & Trust

  • 7 · Data stewardship & sharing culture — data governance policy · named data-steward roles · data-sharing agreements (Home Affairs, Deeds Office) · POPIA processing register
  • 8 · Security, privacy & trust posture — information security policy · POPIA compliance programme · ISO/IEC 27001 control set · security incident register

Citizen & Ecosystem

  • 3 · Citizen digital empowerment — multi-channel access plan · digital-inclusion measures · physical service / housing access points · PEPUDA compliance measures
  • 9 · Partnership & ecosystem — NHFC & HDA partnership agreements · municipal delivery agreements · civil-society / community engagement · PPP instruments

Output 2 — Readiness assessment

Each catalogued dimension is then scored 0–4 and classified into a zone. The scale and level names are different from the architecture's 0–5 scale — the table below explains what each level means and how it maps to a readiness zone.

Enabling environment scoring scale — 0 to 4

Readiness index per dimension = rating ÷ 4 × 100. Zone is set by the index band. Target for this cycle: Established (3). Worst-dimension-wins sets the cluster zone.

RatingLevelWhat it means in practiceIndex /100Zone

1 Nascent Awareness exists and isolated efforts are underway, but capacity is fragile, undocumented and personality-dependent — it evaporates when the individual moves on. 25 Intervene
2 Developing Key roles and some foundations are in place and partly functioning; gaps remain, coverage is incomplete and adoption needs constant enforcement. 50 Improve
3 ★ Established TARGET The capability is reliably institutionalised, fit for purpose and survives changes in leadership. A standing function exists, agreements are enforced and the department can depend on it. 75 Good
4 Strategic The capability is a managed strategic asset — continuously improved, outcome-linked and reused across government to accelerate every dependent initiative. 100 Excellent

Reading the diagram

One spoke stands tall — executive sponsorship (3). Two collapse: procurement agility (1) and, critically, change & delivery capability (1). Leadership wants the change; the machinery to buy and build it does not exist. That mismatch is the single most important finding in the whole assessment.

Why it sits alongside every phase. The architecture assessment (Phases 1–2) and the readiness assessment are combined into the overall PEAR score — 0.6 × architecture + 0.4 × enabling environment = (0.6 × 26) + (0.4 × 47) = 34.4 / 100. A department can have a clear roadmap and still fail, if the environment cannot carry it. GWEA 2.0 forces both to be looked at together.
