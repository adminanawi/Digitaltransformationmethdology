The citizen asks three questions, and the practice answers each from a different element: Am I served? (reach — Element 2 Client), Did my life change? (effect — Element 1 Outcome), and Was it worth the time, travel, documents, money and dignity it took me? (worth — Element 3, Q3.3). Worth is measured net of client burden, cohort-disaggregated, against Batho Pele, the DPI once-only principle and the World Bank time-and-cost method — assessed from the client's own account.

Why "worth" matters, not just reach and effect. A service can reach a citizen and change their life and still cost them three trips, a day of queuing and documents they had to pay to obtain. Q3.3 measures the benefit net of that burden. Eight services bind on gate 7.G2 — no digital self-service channel exists — which is why service digitalisation is deferred to Wave 3, once the systems behind the counter actually exist.
